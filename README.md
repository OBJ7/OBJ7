# Junior Osei Bonsu

I’m a telecommunications engineer based in Ghana. My work at Telecel Ghana involves commercial systems and data operations: SQL, reporting, troubleshooting, and the processes people rely on to get work done.

I’m interested in what happens between a system being designed and someone being able to use it well. Sometimes the problem is in the data or the code. Sometimes it is missing information, an unclear process, or a decision that needs people from different teams. I try to understand the problem before deciding what to build.

Alongside my professional work, I work on opportunity access through APPLY! and practical tools around problems I encounter. I value clear explanations, responsible use of information, and giving collaborators their credit.

## Technical work and learning

My professional foundation includes SQL and operational data work. The public web repositories above show HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and lightweight serverless workflows. I use tools to solve a problem, and aim to explain both their choices and their limits.

[Everyday SQL work](work/sql/README.md) presents two public-safe adaptations of real working artifacts. It separates the operational problem, the query logic and what has actually been verified. [A query is also a definition](notes/a-query-is-also-a-definition.md) explains why counts depend on grain, timestamps and missing information.

The earlier [fictional T-SQL learning lab](operational-data-lab/README.md) remains a separate exercise; it is not a record of employer work or validated production deployment.

I want to understand how people make digital systems workable, and what their adaptations reveal about access, responsibility, and institutional change.
